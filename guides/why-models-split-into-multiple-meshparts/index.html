Why Did My Model Become 3 MeshParts? (The Single-Material Rule & Appearance Grouping)

Understand the Roblox Engine law that gives every MeshPart one surface state—and how MeshMogul merges multi-color parts into one mesh while keeping distinct materials aligned.

Quick Takeaways

  • Why it matters: A single MeshPart in Roblox can hold only one Material, one MaterialVariant, one Transparency, one Reflectance, one DoubleSided flag, and at most one TextureID or SurfaceAppearance .
  • How it affects your creations: Forcing SmoothPlastic, WoodPlanks, and Metal into one MeshPart would strip away two of those materials—whereas untextured parts sharing the same material state can keep different colors via per-corner vertex colors .
  • What you can actually do about it: Let MeshMogul's Appearance Grouping keep incompatible states in aligned outputs sharing one WorldPivot, and clean up accidental Plastic vs. SmoothPlastic or decimal Reflectance drift before baking if you want fewer outputs.

Almost every creator asks the exact same two questions the first time they preview a multi-material build in MeshMogul:

"My market stall had 24 Parts. I clicked Preview Mesh expecting 1 MeshPart—so why did Explorer show a Model containing Output_1, Output_2, and Output_3?"

And right behind it:
"Wait—why did my 12 awning roof stripes (in Red, White, and Blue) merge into one single MeshPart, while my wooden posts and metal brackets split into separate MeshParts?"

Nothing broke, and MeshMogul isn't splitting your build at random. It is obeying a hard architectural rule of the Roblox rendering engine so your asset preserves its materials, reflections, sidedness, and transparency without forcing you to paint external UV texture atlases in Blender.

1. The Roblox Engine Law: One MeshPart = One Surface State

In an external modeling app like Blender, a single mesh object can assign five different material slots to different faces on the same model.

In Roblox Studio, a MeshPart is a single BasePart whose appearance properties govern the entire mesh (Texture Specifications): - One Material (SmoothPlastic, WoodPlanks, Metal, Neon, Glass, etc.) - One MaterialVariant (resolved through MaterialService) - One Transparency and one Reflectance - One DoubleSided setting (true or false) - At most one TextureID / TextureContent or one child SurfaceAppearance

So if a market stall uses SmoothPlastic awning cloth, WoodPlanks framing, and Metal corner brackets—and you aren't baking a custom UV texture atlas externally—those three materials must live on three separate MeshPart instances.

2. How Different Colors Still Merge Into ONE MeshPart

Now for the second mystery: if a MeshPart only has one Color property in the Properties window, how did Red, White, and Blue SmoothPlastic roof stripes merge into one single MeshPart?

Roblox EditableMesh and MeshPart geometry can store per-corner vertex colors directly inside the mesh data: 1. When untextured source parts share the exact same Material, effective MaterialVariant, Transparency, Reflectance, and DoubleSided state, MeshMogul bakes each source part's Color3 into its triangle corners. 2. It assigns neutral white (1, 1, 1) to that output MeshPart.Color so the baked vertex colors render accurately without getting darkened or re-tinted. 3. Because Roblox multiplies vertex colors by the outer MeshPart.Color on untextured meshes, a 100-part SmoothPlastic prop with twenty different colors merges cleanly into 1 MeshPart.

(Important nuance for textured MeshParts: When a source MeshPart already carries a TextureID / TextureContent, moving its outer MeshPart.Color tint into vertex corners is not visually equivalent to native outer tinting—so textured MeshParts keep their native outer Color and only merge when both texture and outer Color match).

3. What Merges vs. What Splits?

When MeshMogul evaluates a supported root, it partitions parts deterministically by compatible appearance state :

Source Comparison Result in MeshMogul 1.3.0 Underlying Engine Reason
Different Colors on untextured Parts (same Material, Transparency, Reflectance, Sidedness) Merges into 1 MeshPart Colors bake into per-corner vertex colors inside the mesh data.
Different Material (e.g., WoodPlanks vs. Metal vs. Glass) Splits (1 MeshPart per Material) One MeshPart supports only one Enum.Material.
Different effective MaterialVariant (or MaterialService base override) Splits (1 MeshPart per effective variant) One MeshPart supports only one MaterialVariant. (Note: mapless variants are supported conditionally; mapped variants with image maps are refused).
Different Transparency (e.g., 0 vs. 0.4) Splits (1 MeshPart per transparency level) Transparency is a whole-part property affecting depth sorting. (Parts with Transparency = 1 are excluded by default).
Different Reflectance (e.g., 0 vs. 0.2) Splits (1 MeshPart per reflectance level) Reflectance is a whole-part BasePart property.
Different DoubleSided on source MeshParts (true vs. false) Splits (1 MeshPart per sidedness state) MeshPart.DoubleSided applies to the entire mesh.
Textured MeshParts (TextureID or SurfaceAppearance) Merges only if texture/SurfaceAppearance and outer Color match; otherwise Splits A MeshPart holds at most one texture/SurfaceAppearance and one outer tint color.

How Grouped Outputs Stay Aligned

Whenever a build produces multiple outputs (Output_1, Output_2, Output_3), MeshMogul builds every output relative to the exact same pivot origin inside one grouped Model whose WorldPivot is locked to that origin . Move, rotate, or reinsert that logical asset from Assets, and all material layers stay locked together.

4. Cleaning Up Accidental Splits in Two Minutes

If Preflight reports 5 outputs on a prop you expected to produce 2, look for accidental property drift across your parts before clicking Preview:

  1. Unify Plastic vs. SmoothPlastic: Under bright lighting, Plastic (which has subtle surface grain) and SmoothPlastic look nearly identical—but to the engine they are two different Enum.Material values! Select your plastic parts and set them consistently to SmoothPlastic (or Plastic).
  2. Zero out stray Reflectance or Transparency decimals: A single brick left at Reflectance = 0.05 or Transparency = 0.01 creates its own extra output group. Reset stray decimals to 0.
  3. Clear stale MaterialVariant names: If a part still carries an old MaterialVariant name that no longer exists in MaterialService, clear that property string so it cleanly uses the built-in material—and remember that destination places must contain matching mapless MaterialVariant definitions if you intend to use custom variants .

5. Two Boundaries Never to Confuse

  • Appearance grouping ≠ Functional component grouping: MeshMogul groups geometry by what it looks like, not by what needs to move. If a wooden door leaf and a wooden doorframe both use WoodPlanks, baking them together fuses the door to the frame! Finalize moving components separately.
  • Why child Decal and Texture overlays are refused: A Decal or Texture instance projects onto a primitive Part's face with per-stud tiling offsets, not a combined MeshPart UV map. MeshMogul refuses selections containing Decal or Texture children rather than silently stripping your artwork .
